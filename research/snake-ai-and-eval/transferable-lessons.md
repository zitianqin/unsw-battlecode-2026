# Transferable lessons for UNSW Battlecode 2026 (ranked)

Each bullet: the lesson → the evidence (file § where it's sourced, with URLs there). **[I]** marks the part that is our inference rather than something a source showed. Ranked by expected impact on results before 1/10/17 Oct, given no model training and an autonomous improve-and-test loop.

## A. Testing loop (highest leverage: every other lesson depends on it)

1. **Never accept a change on <100 games; use SPRT on side-swapped pairs.** 30/50 wins (+70 Elo) has a 95% CI of [−25, +178]; +35 Elo needs ~530 games by SPRT, +14 Elo ~3,300. → testing-methodology.md §1–3 (computed; Fishtest `sprt.py` bounds ln(β/(1−α)) = ±2.944).
2. **Play every (map, seed) twice with sides swapped and score the pair** (Fishtest's pentanomial model "leads to a substantial saving of testing resources"). Starting ids follow map-file order, so one side may systematically move first. → testing-methodology.md §3.
3. **Bounds by regime**: SPRT [0, 70] Elo while changes are big (≈130 games avg), [0, 35] later (≈530), non-regression [−20, 0] for deletions/simplifications. (Replaced 2026-09-24: one bound, [0, 15] normalized Elo with up to 2400 pairs, since the wider bounds rejected real +5 to +20 gains about half the time; since 2026-09-26, [−15, 0] for no-loss changes.) Stockfish uses non-regression tests for simplifications. → testing-methodology.md §3, §7 [bounds for us are I].
4. **Gate against a pool, not just the last version.** Self-play exaggerates gains and cycles (Asymptotic Labs' Battlesnake RL "strategy collapse"; fixed by 20% older opponents and promotion only after 3,000 games vs the 3 previous bests). Agade (CG, 2nd Fall 2020) measured opponent modelling at ~30% win rate in self-play but ~15% vs real players: "self-play evaluation is risky". Pool = champion + older versions + meta archetypes (early 2+2 splitter, greedy grower, turtle, head-on hunter). cgarena's design (candidate vs fixed pool, staged bounded runs, fixed + fresh seeds) is a ready template. → battlesnake.md §2, testing-methodology.md §6, competitive-bot-culture.md §2.
5. **The ladder is too noisy to steer by.** Simulated K=96 / 5-game battles give ~±42 Elo rating SD for a team exactly equal to its opponents. Use the ladder as a slow sanity check and crash detector; decide locally. → testing-methodology.md §6 [calc].
6. **Hold out maps; report per-map.** Known maps + fixed-seed pearls (fixed until 2026-09-25; now seeded per match) invite overfitting; tournament maps may be unseen. → testing-methodology.md §6, research/unsw-game-analysis.md.
7. **Scenario regression tests mined from losses** ("round 154, dragon 7 must not go N"): top Battlesnake devs and Hungry Geese's 2nd place used them; they catch blunders in seconds. → battlesnake.md §8 (Nettogrof), hungry-geese-and-tron.md §1.2 (Kirwin).
8. **A strict staged protocol beats chasing noise**: SnakeByte #91 "strict testing protocol (20→50→100 games) saved us from chasing noise" and logged ~40 failed experiments. Tuners with 20-game fitness (Shapeshifter GA) mostly fit noise [I]. → competitive-bot-culture.md §1.1, battlesnake.md §3.

## B. What to build (bot design)

9. **Exploit sequential turns with real minimax.** Lower ids have already moved (facts); higher ids move after me and see my move (true min nodes). Most snake-AI machinery (paranoid "I move first" pessimism, decoupled bandits, Albatross) exists only because those games are simultaneous. CG Tron, also sequential, used paranoid search with only the nearest rival as a true minimiser and one greedy reply for others. → swarm-coordination.md §4, hungry-geese-and-tron.md §2.3, battlesnake.md §7/§10.
10. **Head-on is a choice made by the later mover.** Moving adjacent to an enemy head that moves after you offers it a trade. Search this exactly; don't assume enemies won't ram (Hungry Geese's 2nd place too often placed 3rd through head-ons with geese its net predicted "would never move towards us"). → swarm-coordination.md §4–5, hungry-geese-and-tron.md §1.2 (rule: `docs/unsw/death.md`).
11. **Trade by value**: a length-2 dragon for a long enemy dragon is a win (especially their longest, which decides the tiebreak); never offer trades with the king. Current meta: most deaths are head-on, 35–47 dragons, longest at r500 only 10–23. → swarm-coordination.md §5 [I on the value model].
12. **Evaluation quality > search depth.** a1k0n won Tron 2010 on eval upgrades (Voronoi nodes+edges fitted by regression on 11,691 replays; chamber tree beat his previous version 12–1); Kirwin's 10× Rust speedup in Hungry Geese gave "only modest gains"; SnakeByte's biggest single gains were a domain-correct distance (gravity-aware BFS). → hungry-geese-and-tron.md §2.1/§1.2, competitive-bot-culture.md §1.1.
13. **Core eval terms, proven in top bots**: multi-source BFS Voronoi on the torus (ties → nobody, because head-on kills both), parity-corrected area (2·min(black, white) + [imbalance]), tails/escape cells in my region, pearls in my region, length diff with diminishing returns, tapered early/late weights (Shapeshifter, 2022's most successful Battlesnake). → battlesnake.md §3, §9.
14. **Time-aware flood fill as the survival floor**: a body cell frees after k tail advances; here the tail cell is still blocked on the turn it would move (`death.md`), and eating delays freeing. "Box smaller than your body" was the recurring killer for 2018's Battlesnake winner. → battlesnake.md §1, §9.
15. **Search only what's local.** Simulate enemy dragons whose heads can reach mine within the horizon; others get a reflex policy (m-schier, Battlesnake 2019). Generate only enough joint enemy moves that each enemy's each move appears once (Shapeshifter). SnakeByte (#1 and #5 used Smitsimax, one search tree per snake): per-snake search + pairing nearby own snakes beat joint search over all own snakes. → battlesnake.md §3, §5; competitive-bot-culture.md §1.1.
16. **Quiescence near heads**: don't evaluate a leaf with an enemy head within 2; extend (Shapeshifter extends ≤5 plies). → battlesnake.md §3.
17. **Iterative deepening on the points counter**, keep the last complete depth; switch to a "hope" search (MCTS/most-escape moves) when minimax says lost (Shapeshifter's MCTS fallback). → battlesnake.md §3.
18. **Cheap first lookahead that won a real contest**: scripted policies × each first move ("prefix moves"), scored by survival + pearls (Graeme Hill, Battlesnake 2018 winner). Good v1 before full minimax. → battlesnake.md §1.
19. **Rule-based bots are competitive at mid-tier; search/eval wins the top.** A pure rules bot reached SnakeByte Legend (#231/2382); a hand-written MCTS reached ~1189 in Hungry Geese, just outside top 10; top places used search + eval (and nets, which we won't train). → competitive-bot-culture.md §1.1, hungry-geese-and-tron.md §1.2.
20. **Only chase pearls you reach first** (Hill's "defensive hungry") = zero-message pearl allocation among teammates by (distance, id). Pearl schedules were predictable on known maps until 2026-09-25; each match now draws a random seed, so plan from the countdowns the bot has seen. → battlesnake.md §1, swarm-coordination.md §2.
21. **Once ahead on the tiebreak, switch to denial + safety.** Snakes AI 2020: the leader circled the apple to deny it and won on time. Our tiebreak is longest dragon, then total length, at round 500. → battlesnake.md §6.

## C. Coordination and comms

22. **Coordinate by running the same deterministic code, not by messaging.** Agreement-critical decisions must use shared inputs only, fixed work (not budget-dependent depth), no per-dragon RNG, canonical tie-breaks by (score, cell, id). → swarm-coordination.md §1.
23. **Authenticate sonar with a keyed tag bound to round + geometry; rotate the key per submission.** Real precedent: in the MIT Battlecode 2020 final, Prasici replayed Steam Locomotive's own first message and sent its units to the map edge; the 1st seed was safe because it changed its "encryption constants" before submitting. → swarm-coordination.md §3.
24. **Bodies are unforgeable messages** (stigmergy): read teammates' facing/position in the 7×7 view before spending sonar. → swarm-coordination.md §2 [I].
25. **Use cheap roles over complex protocols**: MIT Battlecode top teams broadcast a role per unit and compact coordinates (mod 128 on ≤64 maps; log-scale for big numbers). → swarm-coordination.md §3.

## D. Meta-lessons for the autonomous loop

26. **Imitate the ladder before inventing**: public replays are free data. Hungry Geese's 2nd place trained only on top replays filtered by the *weakest* player's rating; a1k0n fitted eval weights from scraped replays. Without nets, replays still give (a) eval-weight fits, (b) archetype bots for the gauntlet, (c) regression scenarios. → hungry-geese-and-tron.md §1.2, §2.1.
27. **Build infrastructure first**: MIT Battlecode veterans' repeated lesson ("working on infrastructure first is always much more beneficial"), and Hungry Geese found evaluation variance was the main bottleneck. → swarm-coordination.md sources (stonet2000 2020 postmortem https://stonet2000.github.io/battlecode/2020/), hungry-geese-and-tron.md §3.

28. **Hand-written bots win short contests; don't plan on learning.** Rule-based/hand-written winners: Halite I–IV, Lux S2, Kore 2022 (Halite IV's winner dropped deep RL after a month). RL won only Lux S1, after months of training. Imitation from ladder replays placed well but never won. Our 3.5 weeks is closer to a 1–2-week CodinGame contest, where search + hand-written eval wins. → competitive-bot-culture.md §3.
29. **Copy what works on the public ladder, fast.** Kaggle winners adopted strong ladder behaviours (Halite IV winner copied a rival's hunting; Kore winner forked the Beta-contest winner). Our replays are public: re-implement top teams' visible tactics as gauntlet opponents first, then as candidate features. → competitive-bot-culture.md §3.
30. **Speed matters, but only after the eval is right.** SnakeByte #5's biggest lever was a faster simulator; #1's was tree structure (merging equivalent states); both on top of a sound eval. C++ is ~60× cheaper than Python in our judge, so write the bot in C++ from the start (brief). → competitive-bot-culture.md §1.1, battlesnake.md §1 (Hill's JS→C++ port).

## Out of scope / leads
- Not researched: Kaggle Rock-Paper-Scissors / Santa; MIT Battlecode 2023/2025 postmortems (downloaded, only grepped); arXiv 2007.10504 and the GI paper on locality search (titles/READMEs only).
- Unverified and worth checking in our own engine: whether lower starting ids (map-file order) give a measurable edge; whether the CLI can generate random maps for hold-out testing (resolved: `mapgen/gen.py`).
- Resolved (sig's decisions, 2026-09-23): fake sonar is allowed; known-map tricks are allowed but ladder-only, and predicting pearls from the fixed seed stopped working on 2026-09-25.
- Tools to port or reuse: Fishtest SPRT/pentanomial code (https://github.com/official-stockfish/fishtest/tree/master/server/fishtest/stats), cgarena's candidate-evaluation design, psyleague.
